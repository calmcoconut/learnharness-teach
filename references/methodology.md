# The method

A teaching session moves the learner from what they already hold to a goal they do not yet hold.
Everything below follows from that.

## 1. Two knowledge states

**Current understanding** is the set of propositions the learner accepts as obviously true. Held
means accepted, self-evident, safe to build on. It does not mean seen, heard, or recitable.

**Goal understanding** is what the learner wants to hold and does not yet. It is often hard to
state; making it concrete is part of the work, not a preliminary.

Measurement brackets the first state: what is held, and where it ends. The goal defines the second.
The edge is the only useful starting point. Teaching below it wastes the learner; teaching above it
is lost on them. A lesson that ignores the edge is a generic lecture, not teaching.

## 2. The terminal condition is obviousness

The session is not finished when the learner can recite the goal, answer questions about it, or when
the material has been covered. It is finished when the goal feels obviously true.

A fact that can be repeated but does not feel obvious is memorized, and memorized facts rot. A fact
that feels obviously true is held. This is also why understanding sticks: the brain commits when the
fact is safe to lock in because nothing more fundamental is likely to contradict it, and connected
because it is held in place by its relations rather than by rehearsal. Feeling obvious is the felt
form of both. Familiarity is not obviousness. A learner can recognize every term, follow every
sentence, and still hold the proposition as an isolated fact.

## 3. The click

Learners arrive holding loose facts: things they know are true but cannot say why. Each is an
unconnected node, held as "that's just how it is."

The click is not the arrival of the final fact. It is what happens to knowledge already held: loose
facts collapse into a structure generated by a few base facts. Each node that previously had to be
held on its own is seen to follow from something more fundamental. The pile becomes a derivation.
Knowing why instead of knowing that is the strongest form of understanding, and the clearest evidence
that structure exists. The goal lands the same way: not as another loose fact, but as a consequence
of the structure.

## 4. Knowledge is a graph

Understanding is a graph: propositions are nodes, reasoning steps are edges. The edges are facts too.
"B follows from A" can be stated, examined, and held. A connection the learner cannot state is not yet
an edge in their model; it is a feeling of coherence that will not survive a question.

A proposition connected to nothing is fragile. A proposition nested in a structure is
self-preserving, because the structure holds it. Teaching is the construction of that structure, not
the transmission of a list.

## 5. One reasoning step at a time

The atomic act of teaching is exposing the learner to a fact. The order is governed by one rule:
move one reasoning step at a time.

Start from what the learner holds. Each new fact attaches to something already held by a single
reasoning step. A single step is small enough to be fully understood at once, and because it follows
from what is held, it inherits the obviousness of its premise.

Two consequences follow. Every landing is self-evident: each step extends the held structure by one
connection, so the new fact lands as "of course." Errors surface immediately: a gap or misconception
reveals itself the moment it is stepped on, not three nodes later under corrupted structure. Because
the step is small, the failure is precisely localized. Repair it before continuing.

"One step" is a claim about inference, not sentence count. If the learner must accept two independent
inferences to reach the new fact, that is two steps. Never build on a fact the learner has not
shown they hold.

## 6. Foundations

Most facts are derived from what is held. Some cannot be. Those need foundations: the bottom of the
dependency tree.

A foundation is accepted as-is. That is why it must be unconditional: simple enough to take at face
value, with no caveats and no hedging. If it needs conditions, it is not a foundation yet; dig
further until you reach something that does not.

Foundations can be motivated. A problem, a question, or a condition that demands the fact makes it
easier to accept. Motivation is not derivation: it does not make the fact follow, but it makes the
fact wanted, and a fact that answers a felt question is easier to hold as self-evident.

There are two placement moves. Derive when the lesson can reach the fact from what is held. Place a
foundation when it cannot. Do not disguise a theorem as a foundation; a wrong root corrupts
everything built on it.

## 7. Two optimizations

### Effort goes into the material

The learner has finite mental effort per session. Optimize its proportion, not its amount. Maximize
direct contact with the material: following steps, answering checks, holding new facts. Minimize
logistics: deciding what to learn, in what order, from what source, checking facts, finding resources,
planning.

The point is not to make the lesson easy. Struggle with the material is how it locks in. The point is
that all of the struggle goes into the material. The teacher absorbs the logistics; the learner
never does.

### The minimal tree

The lesson traverses a dependency structure. The optimization is the minimal tree: the smallest set
of propositions, in the smallest derivation depth, that leads from current understanding to the goal.

Nothing the learner already holds is re-taught. Nothing off the path to the goal is taught, however
interesting. The route is searched in planning, not improvised during teaching. The plan is a coarse
map of that route: structural landmarks, not lesson segments. The reasoning step is the fine unit
actually traversed. Both exist at different resolutions on purpose.

## 8. Presentation

Each step gets its clearest form, chosen per step. Some steps are best stated plainly. Foundations are
often like this: assert them and move on. Most connective tissue belongs in the flow, with the next
fact arriving as the obvious consequence of the last. Announcing the choreography ("now I will
establish X, then connect it to Y") makes the learner process the lesson's structure instead of the
material.

The failure mode is verbosity: performing the method with labeled segments, restated structure, and
meta-commentary instead of doing the work. Every sentence either installs understanding or is
overhead. Explicitness is a tool, not a ritual. The test is always whether the presentation
maximizes absorption.

## 9. Checks

Every reasoning step ends with a check. A check is a graded question verifying that the step landed. It
is not bookkeeping. It is the error-catching mechanism: a step that did not land is found the moment
it is stepped on, precisely localized, and repaired before anything is built on it.

One check per reasoning step. Never a multi-step lecture followed by one check; never several checks
for one step. The check is posed after the teaching and before the next step. A miss means the step
did not land: diagnose, repair, re-establish, and only then continue.

## 10. What this demands

Measure precisely; the quality of the plan is capped by the precision of the measurement. Search the
route; do not guess it. Planning is the highest-leverage work. Teach in single steps, each checked. Do
not let the plan's granularity become the lesson's structure. Do not perform the method; the learner
should experience the material arriving as obvious, not the teacher performing a process.
